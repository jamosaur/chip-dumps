This chip holds every other byte of a 4 MB firmware image for a Hitachi SH-1 microcontroller (SH7032/SH7034 family). It was one half of a 16-bit big-endian pair, and it holds the even-address bytes (D15–D8). Text appears with every second character missing (`Cprgt19 iah,t.` is the even characters of `Copyright 199x Hitachi,Ltd.`). The 114-entry vector table at offset 0 matches the SH7032/7034 vector layout exactly. Opcode high bytes form classic SH sequences such as `D3 43 00` (`mov.l @(disp,PC),R3` / `jsr @R3` / `nop`). After 640 KB of program comes a large asset set: Japanese road-information strings, 8-bit PCM and 4-bit ADPCM voice audio, 4-bpp graphics (a 32-step heading arrow and cartoon mascot animations), an 8×16 half-width font and two JIS X 0208 kanji fonts. The strings mention tunnels, off-ramps, chain restrictions and distances in km. They point to a Japanese in-car navigation or road-traffic-information unit from about 1995–1999.

## Findings

- **Byte lane, stride 2, high byte.** Both strings with even-index characters (`Cprgt19 iah,t.` at 0x0003F3) and opcode statistics support stride 2. The most common bytes in the code are 00, 60, E0, D3, 2F, 8B, A0, which are SH opcode high bytes, and the pair `D3 43` occurs 4503 times (mov.l to R3 then jsr @R3). Bit order is normal: text reads correctly.
- **SH-1 (SH7032/SH7034) vector table at 0x000000.** It has 114 two-byte entries, which are the high bytes 0 and 2 of each 32-bit vector. Power-on PC/SP and manual-reset PC/SP read `0E 40 / 0F FF / 0E 40 / 0F FF`, so the PC is 0x0E?40?? and the SP 0x0F?FF??, the SH-1 on-chip RAM at 0xFFFFxxx. The entries for reserved vectors 73, 75, 77, 79, 83, 87, 91, 95, 99, 110 and 111 all point to the same default handler (`0E 40`). The IRQ0–7, DMAC, ITU0–4, SCI0/1, PRT, A/D, WDT and REF slots have their own handlers, and the table ends at vector 113 (REF CMI), exactly the SH7032/7034 layout. TRAPA vectors 60–63 have dedicated handlers (`0E 1D`, `0E 1C`, `0E 06`, `0E 06`), probably RTOS system-call traps.
- **Address map.** Code pointers all start `0E`. The string table at 0x0A41CE maps to bus 0x0E14839C, and the pointer table at 0x0A4000 holds byte-2 values 0x83 to 0x8C, which matches. The first image at 0x15BCFE maps to 0x0E2B79FC, and the graphics index begins at byte-2 0x7A. So this chip pair is mapped at H'E000000 (16 MB space, area 6 with A27 set on the SH-1). RAM pointers start `09` (H'9000000 region, probably DRAM in area 1). On-chip peripheral addresses `05 FF` / `05 FE` (H'5FFFFxx / H'5FFFExx) occur 286 times in literal pools.
- **The program probably runs under a Hitachi RTOS or C library.** Near 0x0003F3 the copyright text is followed by `IS7H00IC1MV. 93` (also every other character), which is probably an OS or library identifier dated 1993. The trap vectors support an RTOS, but the exact product name cannot be read from one lane.
- **RCS ident at 0x0655DC:** `$d motcv1219/12 13:8kmEpkm$`. These are the even characters of an RCS `$Id: …,v 1.x 199x/x1/2x x1:3x:x8 k?m Exp k?m $` line (a locked revision, so the author's name appears twice).
- **`Mint` records, 0x0655F8 to about 0x06AC9C.** There are 41 records. Each starts with the 4 contiguous bytes `4D 69 6E 74` and is followed by RAM pointers (`09 30`, `09 13`) and small flag words. They are probably UI or task descriptors.
- **String table at 0x0A41CE.** It holds 231 strings, mostly Shift-JIS kanji lead bytes (only lead bytes land on this lane), with half-width katakana that can be read in part. Examples are `ﾄﾈ` (トンネル, tunnel), `ｵﾗﾌ` (オフランプ, off-ramp), `ﾁｰ` ×4 (チェーン, chain restriction), `ｶｽ` (ガス) and `ｾﾀ` (センター). There are distance strings `1k`…`9k` (1–9 km), `10m…` and `1?Kｧ…8?Kｧ`. These look like road traffic information (VICS/JARTIC-style) or navigation message fragments.
- **8-bit PCM at 0x0A4646–0x159668 (about 740 KB on this chip, 1.4 MB on the bus).** It is unsigned, with silence alternating `80`/`7E`. The waveforms are smooth even with every other sample missing. 0x7F is rare (5,334 of about 740,000 bytes), so the encoder may avoid that value. These are probably the voice-guidance phrases.
- **Graphics index at 0x15B900.** A 2-byte field `00 03` is followed by 510 pointers (`0E xx`) to image records. The first 88 records are 0x102 bytes on this chip (0x204 on the bus). Each has a 16-byte prefix (32 bus bytes, probably a 16-entry palette or mask), a 2-byte header `20 00` and then 240 bytes of 4-bpp pixels, which make a 32×30 image of which this lane holds pixel pairs 0–1, 4–5 and so on. Records 0–31 are a 32-step heading arrow in a circle. Records 32–87 are cartoon mascot animations, including sleeping "Zzz" frames. From record 88 (0x1616B2) the data switches to 1-bpp bitmaps.

- **Data header at 0x183000.** The text `FiOt  74:419` followed by `0A` at 0x18302C is the even characters of a C `asctime()` stamp, `Fri Oct  ? ?7:4?:?4 199?`. Fridays with single-digit October dates in 1995 fall on the 6th. At 0x183046, `CpRgtFJTUL MIE 95` is the even characters of a `CopyRight FUJITSU … 1995` line. At 0x183057, `oc yteieD cinr  105H DC eso.` is a second line, also lane-split. Monotonic lookup tables follow from 0x1830A6. This block is probably third-party (Fujitsu) data or middleware bundled into the image.
- **4-bit ADPCM at 0x18B700–0x19B1CA.** It has no autocorrelation and an entropy of 7.2–7.3 bits per byte. Its nibble histogram is sign-magnitude: magnitudes 0 to 6 fall off evenly for both signs (about 13,000 down to about 1,500 per nibble value), and magnitude 7 stands out. This is typical of OKI/Dialogic-style voice ADPCM, which cannot be decoded from half the bytes.
- **Signed-nibble data at 0x19B500–0x1A5000.** Bytes such as `D4 30`, `43 C0` and `25 E0` use nibbles in the range -5 to +5. They are probably delta-coded vector shapes (strokes or map symbols). This is unconfirmed.
- **8×16 half-width font at 0x1A5C70.** It has 256 glyphs in code order: control-code pictures for 0x00–0x1F, ASCII with `¥`, block graphics at 0x80–0x9F, katakana at 0xA1–0xDF, card suits, and the symbols 円年月日時分秒 and 市区町村 at 0xE0 and above. The glyphs are complete on this lane.

![Half-width font](images/halfwidth-8x16-font.png)

- **16-row kanji font, kanji from 0x1ACC8E (亜) onward.** It uses 16 bytes per glyph on this chip, in JIS X 0208 order with non-kanji rows before it. Each byte is the left 8 pixels of a 16-pixel row, so the strokes run off the right edge of the column. The right halves are on the partner chip.

![16-row kanji, left halves](images/kanji-16-row-left-halves.png)

- **12-row kanji font, kanji from 0x1D958E (亜唖娃阿哀…) onward.** It also uses 16 bytes per glyph, but rows 12–15 are always zero. Read 8 pixels wide, these glyphs look complete, and the order is verified against JIS (row 2 of the sheet shows 下化仮何伽価佳加可嘉夏嫁家寡科暇果). It is roughly 6,300 glyphs to about 0x1F2000, so probably level 1 and level 2.

## Device

The best guess, at **medium** confidence, is a Japanese in-car navigation or road-traffic-information (VICS-era) unit from about 1995–1999. The evidence is the road-term strings (tunnel, off-ramp, chain restriction, km distances), the 32-direction heading indicator, the voice phrases stored as both PCM and ADPCM, and the kanji fonts with 市区町村 address symbols. The mascot animations suggest a consumer aftermarket product rather than an OEM head unit. A GPS radar-detector-style product is a less likely alternative. The CPU is an SH-1 (SH7032/SH7034 family) at **high** confidence, from the vector table. The Fujitsu-copyrighted 1995 data block suggests a Fujitsu (possibly Fujitsu Ten) connection, at low confidence. The maker and model cannot be identified from this lane alone.

## Open questions

- **What are the exact OS/library and copyright strings?** Interleave this chip with the low-byte partner to read `Copyright 199x Hitachi,Ltd.`, the `IS7H00IC1MV. 93` identifier and the Fujitsu line in full.
- **What is the reset entry point?** The vector's middle byte is on the partner chip. With both lanes, disassemble from the reset PC (0x0E?40??) with VBR at 0x0E000000.
- **What is the boot path?** The vectors point into H'E000000, not address 0. Look for whether a small boot ROM or the SH7034 on-chip ROM starts this image, or whether the board also decodes it at address 0.
- **What do the strings say?** They are Shift-JIS with only lead bytes on this lane. They need the partner chip to decode.
- **What are the audio formats and sample rates?** Merge both lanes, then play the PCM at 8–16 kHz and try OKI/IMA ADPCM decoders on the 0x18B700 block.
- **What is in the 16-byte image prefix and the `00 03` field?** Test the prefix as a palette (RGB555/565) once the low bytes are available.
- **What does the `Mint` tag mean?** Decode the records once both lanes are present.

## Method

The image was profiled in 64 KB, 8 KB and 2 KB windows (entropy, FFh/00h fractions, byte histograms) with Python, and binwalk was run (no signatures). The stride was found from lane-split ASCII, and the CPU from SH opcode high-byte statistics and the vector-table layout. The base address was confirmed by matching pointer-table byte values against the lane-split offsets of the strings and images they index. Fonts and images were rendered with small PIL scripts, using JIS X 0208 order to find the first kanji. The audio formats were classified from value and nibble histograms and from autocorrelation.
