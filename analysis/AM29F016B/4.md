This 2 MB image holds a firmware set made of chunks. Each chunk starts with a 64-byte header whose magic is `$y` + a type letter + `$`. The chunks run from 0x020000 to 0x0EE004, with zero padding up to the 1 MB mark. The first 128 KB and most of the upper megabyte are erased. Seven chunks have strong 8051-family opcode statistics: `E0`, `90`, `F0` and `A3` are their most common bytes, and pairs like `A3 E0` and `E0 FB` occur at distance 1. The chunk payloads are not stored as plain code, though. Common 8051 sequences are missing, the text shows up only as fragments, and two related modules keep byte-for-byte alignment through small value changes. This points to a byte-aligned compression or transform that I could not identify. Almost all the structure here comes from the chunk headers, which are fully decodable.

## Findings

- **Chunk header (0x40 bytes, little-endian).** The layout is:
  - +0x00: `24 79 tt 24`, the magic, where `tt` is the type letter.
  - +0x04: u32 0.
  - +0x08: u32 count.
  - +0x0D..+0x11: five bytes of the form `xx LL HH LL 00`. For example `$ym$` has `A7 B7 00 B7 00` and `$yl$` has `F8 7E 01 7E 00`.
  - +0x17: one byte that varies between chunks.
  - +0x18: `0B` in every chunk except the `$yb$` group header (`00`).
  - The rest of the header is zero.
- **Count field.** It is 30 (`1E`) in the `$yp$` header at 0x020000 and 1 in the `$yl$` header. In the `$yb$` header it is 11, which exactly matches the number of chunks that follow up to and including `$yi$`. I could not tie the +0x0D..+0x11 bytes or +0x17 to the payload length, a byte sum, an XOR or a CRC-32.
- **The `$yb$` group is stored twice.** The 0x0215EE bytes at 0x09EA12 are identical to the first 0x0215EE bytes at 0x0C0000. The first copy's last chunk (`6` at 0x0BD5C2) has the same header as the complete `6` chunk at 0x0DEBB0 (payload 0xDAF3), but it stops at 0x0C0000. So the first copy was cut short where the second copy begins, on a 64 KB sector boundary.
- **The sector at 0x1E0000 is a stray copy.** It is byte-identical to the 64 KB at 0x09A225, which suggests a sector-sized copy or relocation that was never cleaned up.
- **Zero padding.** Everything from 0x0EE004 to 0x0FFFFF is `00`, not `FF`. That suggests a 1 MB image file, padded with zeros, was programmed starting at 0x000000 or 0x020000.
- **Seven 8051-family modules.** In `m`, `t`, `p`, `4`, `4`, `p` and `i`, the most common bytes are `E0 90 F0 A3`. At distance 1, the dump has `A3 E0` ×396 against `E0 A3` ×30, `E0 FB` ×608 against `FB E0` ×51, and `90 FF` ×332 against `FF 90` ×185. So the byte order is forward 8051 code.
- **Common 8051 module prefix.** All seven modules have `43 57 F0 00 00 00 00 34 34` at payload offset +0x0A, for example at 0x09EA9C and 0x0A046C. In `m`, `t` and `i`, the bytes before it alternate with `FF`, for example `01 3C FF 30 FF 5D FF 20 FF 36`.
- **The payloads are not plain code.** `E4 F0` (CLR A; MOVX @DPTR,A) appears only 15 times in 180 KB. There are runs that real code would not contain: 13 × `90` at 0x0A225B and five `74` bytes at 0x09EBBD. No English word appears in order anywhere in the image; I searched for `error`, `Error`, `ver`, `Copy`, `http` and their reversals. Bit-permutation and address-line-permutation searches did not improve readability over the stored order. The checks also ruled out raw zlib/deflate, LZMA-alone and plain BWT blocks: the inverse-BWT LF mapping never forms a single cycle.
- **Probably a byte-aligned compressor.** Module `i` (0x0EC723) and module `m` (0x09EA92) have identical payloads for about the first 0x3E0 bytes, apart from single-byte value changes. Examples are `E5 5D`→`E5 5A`, `5F 60`→`5C 60` and `64 F0`→`61 F0` (each lower by 3), plus others such as `03 F0`→`04 F0`. After the first insertion they drift apart into short matches. That is how literal bytes behave in an LZ-style stream, so `i` is probably a revised build of `m`.
- **Second code type.** The 8 KB at 0x020140 and the two `6` chunks share a byte profile: `00 2E FF 80 4F 02 6E 78 FC`, with `5E 56` 204–331 times per chunk and `2E 00` up to 1514 times. Common signatures of 68k, ARM, Thumb, MIPS, SH and H8 do not occur, so the architecture is unknown. The data is probably compressed or transformed the same way.
- **Scrambled text in the first chunk.** The fragments look like format strings and identifiers:
  - `s20%:u20%/u20%` at 0x020B3E reads as `%02u/%02u:%02s` backwards.
  - `X00  X20  X40  X60` at 0x020B84.
  - `uvrICEO%dCIR MCU` at 0x021A00.
  - `WM$at0whWM$at0wh` at 0x021A89.
  - `spiecXpelErnps` at 0x021F49, which may be from `Exception`.
  - `21MFBD4` at 0x02163C, `20A90MFBD1` at 0x021644 and `4544N20-N20MFAD0` at 0x020627, which may be flash part-number fragments.
  - `MP8S` at 0x021608 and `P3MI` at 0x021615.
- **Possible end-of-image checksum.** The last four bytes before the zero fill, `10 57 65 F8` at 0x0EE000, are probably an image checksum.
- **Byte order and data lines.** The count field is little-endian (`1E 00 00 00`). ASCII and 8051 opcode values come through unchanged, so the data bits are not swapped.

## Device

Best guess, with low confidence: a consumer embedded device whose firmware is split between an 8051-family controller and a second processor or DSP of unknown architecture. Chinese DVD, VCD or MP3 players and similar AV products often combine an 8032 with a media processor and keep compressed modules in parallel NOR. That fits the module mix and the `MP3`-like fragments, but nothing in the image names a maker or product. The erased first 128 KB means this chip does not hold a boot loader that runs straight from flash. Either a boot ROM elsewhere loads and unpacks these chunks, or the boot block was erased.

## Open questions

- **What is the compression or transform?** The next step is to find the code that reads `$y?$` chunks. It would be in an uncompressed boot ROM or loader from the same board. Another route is to try more LZ variants that keep literal bytes, such as LZ variants with split literal and token streams, starting at payload offset +0x13, just after `34 34`.
- **What do the header bytes +0x0D..+0x11 and +0x17 mean?** Test them against the uncompressed module sizes once a decoder exists. Also try CRC-16 or Fletcher variants over header plus payload.
- **What is the second architecture?** Decompress a `6` chunk first, then check the result for prologue and return patterns. The frequent `5E 56` and `2E 00` are the best leads.
- **Why is the `$yb$` group stored twice, and why the stray copy at 0x1E0000?** It may be an interrupted update or a sector-copy scheme. Another dump from the same device type would show whether the layout is fixed.
- **What is the 256-byte table at 0x020040?** It is mostly zero, with values only at indices 0x00 and 0x24–0x2C. It might be a symbol or flag table for the decoder.

## Method

I used Python 3 (`-I`, scratch scripts in a temp dir) and `xxd`, `strings` and `binwalk` (no signatures found). The steps were:

1. Per-sector and per-window entropy, plus `FF`/`00` fill maps.
2. A regex scan for the `$y?$` magic, then header decoding and byte comparison of the duplicated ranges.
3. Byte-pair statistics against 8051 opcode pairs, and diffing of the related modules `m` and `i` with `difflib`.
4. Negative tests for the encoding: brute-force searches over address-bit permutations (low 4–13 bits, including GF(2)-linear maps on 4 bits) and data-bit permutations, scored with an English bigram model; inverse-BWT cycle tests over many block sizes and boundaries; zlib, deflate and LZMA trial decompression at every early payload offset.

I rendered the chunk map with PIL from the decoded chunk table.
