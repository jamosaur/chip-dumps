This chip holds every eighth byte of a 64-bit-wide flash array, and that array stores three complete TrueType fonts back to back. The chip has lane 7 of the eight. The strongest evidence is at `0x080000`, where this lane shows part of an sfnt table directory. The odd bytes are the last letter of each table tag (`2 p ␠ m f d a x a p e t p T`, which reads as OS/2, cmap, cvt , fpgm, glyf, head, hhea, hmtx, loca, maxp, name, post, prep and a 14th tag ending in T). The even bytes are the low bytes of the table offsets, and all of them are multiples of 4. The bulk of the image is glyph outline data. Its byte histogram is dominated by TrueType point flags (`01`, `06`, `07`, `15`, `23`, `33`, `35`…), and long coordinate deltas show up as high bytes `FE`, `FD`, `01` and `02`. Reconstructed name-table fragments read `Copyright (c) 1994 Monotype Typography Ltd` and carry Windows language IDs whose low byte is `04` (Chinese).

## Findings

- **Stride is 8 bytes, not 2 or 4.** The name tables show it. In font 1 the record bytes after `0x0C60DD` run `01 67 00 | 01 06 00 | 01 0D 00 | 01 0D 00 | 03 8C 04 | 03 0C 04 | 03 0A 04 | 03 1A 04`. Each triple is platform-ID low byte, string length low byte, then the *next* record's language-ID low byte. With 12-byte records, only a stride of 8 produces that order, and it sees only every second record. The Windows lengths are twice the Mac ones (`05`→`0A`, `0D`→`1A`), as expected for UTF-16. In font 2 the lane falls on the name header's count low byte, which is `10` (16 records).
- **Lane 7 of 8.** Chip byte `0x080000` = `80` matches file byte 7 of an sfnt header (searchRange = 128 for 14 tables). Chip byte 1 = `'2'` is file byte 15, the last character of the first tag, OS/2. The OS/2 offset LSB is `EC`, which equals 12 + 14×16, so the font has exactly 14 tables and OS/2 is stored first.
- **maxp confirms the model.** Just before each name table the four visible maxp bytes are `E0 02 00 00` (font 1) and `4D 02 00 00` (font 3). Those are maxPoints LSB, maxZones = 2 (always 2), maxInstructionDefs = 0 and maxComponentDepth = 0. In font 2 the lane falls on the other phase and reads `00 00 05 3D` (version, maxCompositePoints, maxStorage, maxSizeOfInstructions).
- **Fonts 2 and 3 start 4 bytes off the 8-byte grid.** At `0x0C616C` and `0x12F414` the lane reads `00 60`: the version LSB, then rangeShift = 0x60 (14 tables). After that come 14 pairs of (checksum LSB, length LSB):
  - Font 2 lengths: `4E 36 5A 46 03 D4 36 24 80 84 20 11 20 7A`
  - Font 3 lengths: `4E 36 62 34 03 CC 36 24 F8 FC 20 F0 20 08`

  The pairs fit the sorted tag order OS/2 (78 bytes, version 0), PCLT (54), cmap, cvt , fpgm, glyf, head (54), hhea (36), hmtx, loca, maxp (32), name, post (32), prep. The fpgm entry (checksum LSB `70`, length LSB `03`) is the same in both, which suggests a shared vendor fpgm. Font 1 has the same 14 tables, but its T-ending tag (very probably PCLT) is stored last.
- **Bus positions under the lane-7 model:** font 1 at 0x400000, font 2 at 0x630B64, font 3 at 0x97A0A4, and data ending near 0xC8EAEF. That gives font sizes of about 2.30 MB, 3.45 MB and 3.23 MB, which is normal for CJK TrueType fonts.
- **Glyph counts from loca** (long format: the LSBs are all even, and the first entries `00 44 44` match a 68-byte .notdef followed by the empty .null, CR and space glyphs). Font 1 has 7,712 glyphs, font 2 has 7,712 and font 3 has 7,806. Each lane byte covers two loca entries. In font 3 the last visible entry is `CC`, which matches the glyf length LSB in its directory. This many glyphs fits GB2312 (7,445 characters) plus Latin and extras.
- **Glyph data.** The byte histogram over 0x080180–0x18FE80 peaks at TrueType flag values: horizontal strokes `23`/`33`/`22`/`32`, vertical strokes `15`/`35`/`14`/`34`, and both-long `01`. Common trigrams include `34 33 14` and `16 15 14`. `FF` is rare (618 times in 1.1 MB) while `FE` and `FD` are common. That is what you expect from TrueType long deltas, because values of ±255 or less are stored short. Coordinates, headers and endPts are split across the other lanes, so outlines cannot be rendered from this chip alone.
- **cmap ramps.** The small-table areas contain arithmetic runs that are lane samples of 16-bit arrays. Examples are `70 68 60 … 08 00 F8 F0 …` (step −8, repeated three times) at `0x0C6200` and `0x12F4A5`, `02 06 0A … BE` (step 4) at `0x0C6253`, and `00 01 08 10 … 60` at `0x0C61A0`. They look like cmap format 4 idRangeOffset and glyph-ID arrays seen one entry in four.
- **Name strings, sampled.** These are Mac strings seen every 8th byte and Windows UTF-16 strings seen every 4th character:
  - Font 1 Mac: `o 4prdi it.geeup HdHd g rnyy`. It fits `Copyright (c) 1994 Monotype Typography Ltd…` exactly at positions 1, 9, 17, 25, 33, 41. The family/subfamily lengths (6, 6) and the H/d positions fit a name like `MHeiGB` / `Medium` / `MHeiGB-Medium` (13 characters). The trademark string ends in `…Monotype Typography`.
  - Font 2 Mac: `i nyyo jS l dMnaiB2BMaedfprd` (the `74` before it is the last record's length), which again has the `Monotype Typography` pattern. The family name is 6 characters and the trademark is 58.
  - Font 3: ` nyy Pvi.LonhB.GMiitkop c9Mt opL c9Pevoi .…`. The Mac copyright (66 characters) fits `© 199x Monotype Typography…`. The Windows copyright fits `(c) 199x Monotype Typography L…`. A 7-character family is followed by a 5-character subfamily starting with `L`, consistent with `Light` and with a name like `MSungGB` (13-character full and PostScript names).
- The 13 bytes at `0x000000` are the only data in the first 4 MB of this lane's address space. The fonts start exactly at bus 0x400000, so this is probably a small module header or font index in front of them.

## Device

Best guess: a Chinese font module or font board for a laser printer, built as a 16 MB array of eight 2 MB flash chips on a 64-bit data path. It holds Monotype's GB-encoded Simplified Chinese TrueType fonts, very probably members of the MHei and MSung families (Monotype sold these in GB encoding). Confidence: **medium**.

- High confidence: the image holds Monotype Chinese TrueType fonts, and this chip is one lane of eight.
- Medium confidence: it came from a printer. Every font carries a PCLT table, the PCL printer-language metadata table, and a 64-bit-wide store of raw TrueType files is typical of printer controllers and their font DIMMs.
- Low confidence: the exact printer maker and the exact family names, which rest on 1-in-8 samples of the strings.

## Open questions

- **Exact font names and versions.** These need the other seven lanes. Interleaving the eight chips byte by byte, with this chip as byte 7, should give three valid TTF files starting at bus 0x400000, 0x630B64 and 0x97A0A4. Their checksums can then be verified against the directory LSBs listed here.
- **The 104-byte header at bus 0x000000.** It needs the partner lanes. Check whether it lists the three font offsets and sizes.
- **Bus width or interleave.** Eight lanes on a 64-bit bus and two interleaved 32-bit banks of four chips look the same from one chip. The board layout or the partner chips would settle it.
- **Font 1's 14th table** ends in `T` and sorts last. Read the full tag from lanes 4–6 of the directory to confirm it is PCLT.

## Method

I used Python (`python3 -I`) on a scratch copy. The steps were: per-sector entropy and erased-byte maps; binwalk (no hits); printable-run and mutual-information tests at strides 1, 2 and 4 (no structure); byte and trigram histograms, which matched TrueType glyph flags; and per-block statistics to find the glyf, hmtx and loca regions. The 8-byte stride and lane came from decoding the name-record triples, the sfnt directory at `0x080000` (tag last letters and offset LSBs) and the maxp bytes. The name strings were reconstructed by aligning the sampled characters against candidate Monotype copyright and trademark text. A web search confirmed that Monotype sells GB-encoded MHei and MSung Chinese families.
