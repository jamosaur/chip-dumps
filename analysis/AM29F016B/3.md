This chip held one byte of each 32-bit word of a 68000-family program ROM, so it is one of four chips. The dump has also been read badly. Every byte equals the byte 0x800 away (`i == i ^ 0x800` for all 2,097,152 bytes, no exceptions), so address line A11 never changed during the read and only 1 MB of the 2 MB is really here. The data that did survive is laid out like this. The image opens with a lane of a 68k vector table. A wide stretch of code follows, in which the bytes of epilogue and argument-push sequences recur once per 32-bit word (`4C 4E`, `0C 66`, `48 48 48 … 4E`). Next come multilingual upper-case strings in fragments, numeric tables and a complete 8×8 font with accented Latin letters and `¿`. Then about 1 MB of 8-bpp bitmaps with a 128-byte row period: on a 32-bit bus that is 512 pixels per row, and some screens show three-reel layouts. The top 256 KB is unsigned 8-bit PCM sound. Taken together, this points to a video gaming or slot machine (AWP) built around a 68020-class CPU and aimed at Spanish- or Latin-language markets.

## Layout

Offsets are chip offsets. Multiply by 4 for the CPU address, assuming the ROM is mapped at 0. Throughout the image, only the first 2 KB of every 4 KB block holds readable data, and the second 2 KB is a copy of it.

## Findings

- **Read fault on A11.** All 1,048,576 byte pairs `(i, i^0x800)` match, and no other address bit comes close (the next best is bit 0, 62%). Even erased regions and the high-entropy audio repeat exactly, so this is a reading fault (a pin without contact, a bent pin or an open adapter trace), not how the chip was programmed. The vector table sits at 0x000000 as expected, which suggests the readable halves are the A11 = 0 ones. That is not proven.
- **Byte lane of a 32-bit bus, stride 4.** The code area contains only 26 `4E 75` (RTS) and 13 `4E 56` (LINK), far too few for a complete 68k image. Instead it holds 336 `4C 4E`, 130 `0C 66`, 7,736 `2F 2F` and 59 `4E 4E 48`. These are the opcode high bytes of 4-byte instructions in consecutive 32-bit words: `movem.l (sp)+,…` then `rts`, `cmpi` then `bne.w`, runs of `move.l …,-(sp)`, and `rts`, `link`, `movem.l …,-(sp)` at function boundaries. Runs such as `48 48 48 48 48 48 48 48 4E` (eight `pea #n.w`, then `jsr`) only fit stride 4.
- **Text confirms the stride.** Letter pairs in 0x030000–0x037FFF match English letter pairs four characters apart (cosine 0.885) better than adjacent pairs (0.676), pairs two apart (0.744) or three apart (0.834). Strings come out as fragments: `LOING` at 0x03201E, `%%%d%%d:` at 0x03202B, `FDTPOD.AICRDP7-PWGCMONL.NCALIT S` three times from 0x036522, `TMONL 14/` at 0x036472. Letter frequencies (E, A, R, O, T, I, N, then high C, U, P with few H) suggest Spanish, Italian or Portuguese text alongside English.
- **Lane position (likely D15–D8, lane 2).** Opcode high bytes rule out lanes 1 and 3. In the vector table, vector 1 (initial PC) reads `0A` and most other vectors read `95`. On lane 0 that would put the PC at 0x0Axxxxxx and every handler at 0x95xxxxxx, which is implausible. On lane 2 it means a common default handler at 0x….95xx and a reset PC of 0x….0Axx. Vectors 64–72 and 128/144/160/176 differ (`81 81 8C 95 8C 18 4C 95 27`, `4D 4E 4E 4F`). These are probably the interrupt and trap handlers in use.
- **CPU family.** 68k with a 32-bit data bus, so a 68020/68EC020/68030/68040 or a 32-bit ColdFire. The lane does not show which.
- **8×8 font at 0x054048.** The font is stored whole in this lane, 8 consecutive bytes per glyph. It covers ASCII from space to `~` (a few glyphs such as `#`, `K`, `_` are blank), then accented capitals and lower case in ISO 8859-1 order, `¿`, `ß`, `¢`, `£`, `©`, `®` and `™`. Because a whole font sits in one lane, the CPU must read it at a stride of 4, or the other lanes hold their own fonts.

![8x8 font](images/font-8x8-latin1.png)

- **Ramp tables at 0x050000.** `01 03 05 … FD FF` is followed by a slightly curved copy (`… 2D 2E 31 33 35 36 …`). At stride 4 these are the low bytes of 16-bit table entries, perhaps colour or volume curves.
- **Record table at 0x03C084.** Groups of 5 bytes such as `00 04 09 36 09`, `00 04 09 3F 12`, `00 04 09 48 1B`, `00 06 0C 5E 31` have a running total in the last two fields. Each group is one lane of a 20-byte record, possibly a paytable or offset index.
- **Graphics 0x060000–0x153FFF.** Autocorrelation peaks at a lag of 128 (0.84–0.92 in several blocks), so these are 8-bpp bitmaps with 128 bytes per row in this lane, 512 pixels per row on the CPU. Rendered with an arbitrary palette, they show full-screen layouts: three tall reel windows at 0x080000 and 0x08C000, and arcs of symbols over three coin-like icons and buttons at 0x0B8000. Some blocks have a different row width (they render as diagonal stripes) and are probably sprites or symbol strips.

- **Audio 0x1C0000–0x1F9B96.** Unsigned 8-bit PCM: the mean is 127–130 in every block and the end fades to a flat `80 80 80` before erased flash. Whether the samples are contiguous here or one sample in four cannot be decided from this lane.

## Device

The best guess is a video slot or AWP gaming machine with a 68020-class CPU and four 2 MB flash chips forming an 8 MB 32-bit program/graphics/sound ROM. Confidence is **medium** for the type of machine and **low** for make and model. The evidence: 512-pixel 8-bpp screens with three reel windows and coin graphics, PCM sound effects, a font with Spanish `¿` and other Latin-1 letters, and many short abbreviated strings with `%d` counters. That combination fits a gaming machine for Spain, Portugal or Latin America. No maker name, copyright or version string survives in this lane.

## Open questions

- **Re-dump.** Half of the chip is missing. If the physical chip is still available, re-read it after checking the A11 pin and the adapter trace for its package. Verify that `i` and `i ^ 0x800` then differ.
- **Exact lane.** Lane 2 rests on the vector-table reading alone. Interleaving with the other three lanes would confirm it, by rebuilding whole 68k vectors and code.
- **Header text at 0x000100.** The 16-byte records with 8 text bytes each are probably copyright, version or date strings, spread over four lanes. They need the partner chips.
- **Palette.** The renders use false colours. Search the partner lanes, or this lane's tables at 0x038000–0x041FFF, for a 256-entry palette.
- **Data at 0x052000 and 0x1F8000.** These look like 1-bpp glyph slices and a structured table respectively. Both need the other lanes, or the code, to decode.

## Method

Python (with `-I`) for per-sector and per-4 KB entropy, `FF`/`00` maps and a scan of address bits for duplication, which found A11. Byte-pattern counts of 68k opcodes, letter-pair statistics of the text region against English at distances 1 to 5, and a hill-climb over address-bit permutations ruled out scrambling within 2 KB. Autocorrelation found the bitmap row period. The renders were made with PIL. binwalk found no known signatures. The code was not disassembled, because only one byte of each 32-bit word is here.
