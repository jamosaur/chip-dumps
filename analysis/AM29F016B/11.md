This 2 MB image holds only one byte of each bus word from a wider flash array, not a self-contained file. The clearest evidence is in the header tables. One table counts up by `9Dh` per entry and wraps past `FFh` several times, which only makes sense for the low byte of wider values. In the matching slot of the second block, the same table is a constant `40h`. A short run of ASCII text near the end keeps a normal character set under the identity bit order, but reads as jumbled letters (`1M p 9o nHmed-.-H re ea.`), as expected when only every second or fourth character is present. The used area holds two blocks with the same layout. Each has a header of step tables, a long packed-data body, a sorted lookup table of exactly 14,493 entries, a 29-byte record list and a few strings. The first 512 KB is erased apart from a 13-byte record at offset 0. No CPU code was found.

## Findings

- **Byte-lane split.** The block 1 header has a 92-entry table at 0x080090: `00 9d 3a d7 74 11 ae 4b e8 85 22 bf …`. It steps by `9Dh` modulo 256 and wraps about 56 times, which only makes sense for one byte of wider values. In the same slot of block 2 (0x0FD96A) the table is `00` followed by 92 bytes of `40h`, so the lower bytes of those values must sit on other chips. The body has no stride-2, 4 or 8 structure: per-lane entropy is 7.03–7.04 bits/byte for every phase. That means all bytes here belong to the same lane, not that the image is a whole file.
- **Text confirms the split and the bit order.** The strings at 0x0FD891 (`1M p 9o nHmed-.-H re ea. 1 oepa .(1-Pevoi .`, `dMt  i 2TidM age drfnpyryd`) and 0x1BEF04 (`1M p -eo noShLELnremM p`) use only letters, digits, spaces and punctuation. Of all 40,320 data-bit permutations, the identity gives the most English-like character mix, so the data lines are wired normally. The words are scrambled because this chip holds only every second or fourth character. Both string sets start with `1M p`.
- **Fields that alternate with zero.** The block 1 header begins `01 03 44 00 29 00 b7 00 1f 3c f5 00 e3 00 49 00 59 00 e4 00 1b 00 03 00 02 00 f4 00`. Every second byte is zero, which fits 32-bit fields split across two bus words (16-bit bus) or pairs of fields. The block 2 header (0x0FD8DC) begins with count `0c` and 12 pairs `53 00 43 00 6d 00 6c 00 65 5e 68 5e 6d 5e 6f 5f 61 60 61 60 6f 60 72 60`. The first byte of each pair is a letter (`S C m l e h m o a a o r`); the second is non-decreasing (`00 00 00 00 5e 5e 5e 5f 60 60 60 60`).
- **The two blocks share one format.** The header tables sit at the same relative offsets in both blocks:
  - +0x032: 12 entries (`06 0e … 5e`) in block 1, 11 entries (`0a 12 … 5a`) in block 2, both step 8.
  - +0x076/0x077: step `20h` tables, `10 30 … f0` ×3 in block 1 and `00 20 … e0` ×3 in block 2.
  - +0x08F/0x090: the 92-entry table described above.
  - +0x0F3/0x0F4: two step-4 runs. Block 1 has `05 … 9d`, eight zeros, then `a3 … fb` (39 + 23 entries). Block 2 has `03 … 9f`, eight zeros, then `a1 … fd` (40 + 24 entries).
  - The body starts at +0x13B/0x13C in both.
- **Sorted tables of the same length.** Both tables are exactly 0x389D (14,493) bytes long and never decrease. The block 1 table at 0x0F9FD7 is 7,554 zeros (with a single stray `d5` at 0x0FB2AF), then values climbing to `3Ch`. The block 2 table at 0x1BB64A is 7,489 zeros, then values climbing to `5Eh`. The run length for each value shrinks slowly and jumps back up at a few points (block 1 at `17h` and `3Bh`, block 2 at `24h`), so the full values form a piecewise curve. This lane must be a high-order byte, because a low byte would wrap. In both blocks the table is preceded by `01 01 00 xx 00 00 00` and a 48-byte table whose values cluster near multiples of a fixed step: about 256/9 in block 1 (`1d 39 56 73 aa c7 e3`) and about 256/12 in block 2 (`14 2b 3f 56 6a 96 aa c1 d5 ec`).
- **Record list before the strings.** Block 1 (0x0FD874): `01 15 00 00 00 00 00 01 | 00 4e 03 | 00 67 05 | 00 77 07 | 04 bc 01 | 04 5e 03 | 04 90 05 | 04 a4 07`. Block 2 (0x1BEEE7): `01 17 00 00 00 00 00 01 | 00 4d 03 | 00 65 05 | 00 75 07 | 04 bb 01 | 04 57 03 | 04 89 05 | 04 9b 07`. The third byte repeats 3, 5, 7, 1, 3, 5, 7 and the first byte splits the entries into a group of three (`00`) and a group of four (`04`). The middle bytes differ only slightly between the blocks, so they are likely one lane of offsets into the string area that follows.
- **Body data.** The bodies have no compression or filesystem signatures (binwalk finds nothing) and no readable text. They alternate between short runs (typically 8–14 bytes, about every 38 bytes) of a restricted alphabet and stretches of near-random bytes. In the restricted runs bits 3, 6 and 7 are clear, so values look like `01 21 11 15 35 07 27 23 37 33 06 16 26 36`. The bytes `FE`, `FD` and `FC` are unusually common in the random stretches. This looks like variable-length records or a packed stream with small fields. No CPU vector table, prologue pattern or code-like byte distribution was found.

## Device

Unknown; confidence **low**. The chip is one lane of a data flash bank, probably two or four AM29F016B in parallel, and it holds no program code. The two blocks share one container format, with a fixed 14,493-entry lookup table and a short name list each. That pattern suggests a product that stores interchangeable content sets, such as two tunes, voice banks, levels, patterns or designs, alongside firmware kept elsewhere. The isolated 13-byte record at offset 0, in an otherwise erased first 512 KB, looks like a small settings or directory record written by the device. Nothing here names a product or manufacturer.

## Open questions

- **Bus width (16 or 32 bits) and lane index.** The fields that alternate with zero fit 32-bit values split over a 16-bit bus, but they do not prove it. Next step: interleave this image with candidate partner lanes and check the `9Dh` table and the strings.
- **Format of the bodies.** The bodies cannot be decoded from one lane. Next step: once the partner lanes are found, check whether the restricted-alphabet runs are pixel or sample data and the `FEh`/`FDh` bytes are markers.
- **Meaning of the 14,493-entry table.** The full-width values are needed. Next step: rebuild the table from all lanes and plot it. The run-length jumps suggest a piecewise curve or a sorted offset index into the body.
- **Strings.** They should become readable once interleaved with the other lanes, and would probably name the two blocks.

## Method

Python (stdlib, numpy and Pillow) and binwalk, run on a read-only copy. Steps: entropy and fill per 64 KB sector, then a list of runs of repeated bytes, which exposed the two sorted tables of equal length. Next, a search for modulo-256 arithmetic progressions in the block headers, and per-lane entropy and autocorrelation at strides 2, 4 and 8. Finally, all 40,320 data-bit permutations were scored against English character frequencies on the trailer strings, and the two blocks were compared at matching relative offsets. The two images were rendered from the raw bytes.
