This chip is one byte lane of a 32-bit flash bank built from four 8-bit chips, so it holds every fourth byte of the data. The first 4.3 KB has low entropy: a short header, about 3.5 KB of structured binary (code or tables), and two blocks of text that cannot be read in one lane. From 0x00113B to the last byte of the chip there is high-entropy payload with no erased space. Three pieces of evidence tie it to dump 8, which another analysis showed to be one lane of a four-chip bank with data bits D0 and D2 cross-wired. First, the same D0↔D2 swap shows up in this chip, both in its text and in the bit statistics of its payload. Second, the text block at 0x0010D3 shares lane-split fragments with the diagnostic reports in dump 8, byte for byte (`hdurh:wl+/hp` at 0x001113 here and at 0x10D8F4 and 0x112B3D in dump 8). Third, the payloads of both chips have the same unusual statistical fingerprint. The two chips came from the same kind of board running the same firmware, but they are not lanes of the same bank, because their layouts differ. The device itself is unidentified.

## Findings

- **Fill.** The chip is fully used. No sector is erased, and no run of 8 or more identical bytes exists anywhere in the payload (0x00113B–0x1FFFFF). Every 64 KB sector from 0x01 to 0x1F has 7.97–7.99 bits/byte. Only the first 4 KB of sector 0 has structure.
- **Data bits D0 and D2 are swapped.** Searching all bit permutations of the text at 0x000E33–0x00113A for the best fit to English letter frequencies, the top result is the D0↔D2 swap. Raw `q`, `v` and `k` become `t`, `s` and `n`, and the strings gain plausible vowels (`eps leps`, `hm hp ha=`). The payload confirms it independently. Within each byte of payload A, as read, bit 0 correlates with bits 1 and 3 (+2.6% and +2.7%) and bit 2 only with bit 1. After swapping D0 and D2 only neighbouring bits correlate, in an unbroken chain b0–b1–…–b7 at +2.6% to +2.8%. Dump 8 shows the same pattern as read. This is the same wiring quirk that dump 8's analysis found.

![Bit correlation before and after the swap](images/bit-correlation-d0-d2-swap.png)

- **The text is split four ways.** No bit permutation turns the text into readable words, so the chip holds only part of each string. A bigram test against about 3 MB of program strings rates stride 1 (log-likelihood −4.15 per pair) and stride 2 (−3.76) clearly worse than stride 4 (−3.59), which fits characters that are four apart in the original. The strings keep regular structure, for example seven 7-byte fields starting `dl+` at 0x000F66 (raw), four of them `dl+d   `, and `hm hp ha=` / `hz hn hf hdi=` (swapped) at 0x000F20. That points to tables of column headings or format strings spread across four chips. Dump 8's text was shown to be stride 4 by an exact fit of hex-dump columns, and this chip comes from the same board type.
- **Text shared with dump 8.** The block at 0x0010D3 (raw `WG-lzWDF0-` LF `CI2!_Q!P!_V!…)IECRc` LF `hdurh:wl+/hp` LF `|hf<2 fovvkFl  D00 nm!_!-m`) contains fragments that recur in dump 8's report records 1, 3 and 4. Examples are `hdurh:wl+/hp` (dump 8 offsets 0x10D8F4 and 0x112B3D), `|hf<2` (0x110229, 0x112750), `IECRc` (0x10DCE8, 0x11060D) and `0od(` (three times). Matching lane strings this long happen only when both chips hold the same lane phase of the same text. The firmware probably stores this text here and copies it into each report, for example as a banner or version string.
- **Repeated text block.** The 44 bytes at 0x000FFC (raw `F(,< 45l rd`, `FG 520 vpev`, `:K2+06(f5ieG U+604fo e,`) occur again at 0x001028. In four-lane terms that is about 176 bytes of text stored twice, likely a copyright or version banner. Digits such as `520`, `2+06` and `604` survive in the lane.
- **Header.** The first 20 bytes hold `0E 53 FF 00` and the six printable bytes `3lz2dk`. In a striped bank each of these is one byte of a separate 32-bit word, so the fields cannot be read. Dump 8's record headers (`BA 00 xx 00` plus an unstriped 7-character name) look different.
- **Structured head.** 0x000380–0x000630 holds about 40 copies of a 9-byte pattern `FF 02 00 00 xx 00 06 00 00` (raw), where `xx` steps slowly through `F4`, `F0`, `EF`, `EB`, `ED`, `E9`, `EE`, `EA`, `EC`. Spread over four lanes, that is a 36-byte record repeated with one changing field, like a table or an unrolled sequence of calls. 0x0002B0 holds nine copies of `80 BE 00 F8` (raw). The byte histogram does not match the opcode byte of common 32-bit CPUs. PowerPC (`7C`, `90`, `94`, `38`), MIPS (`8F`, `AF`, `27`) and ARM (`E5`, `E1`) opcode bytes are all nearly absent. This lane therefore holds operand or displacement bytes, or data, and the CPU cannot be determined.
- **The payload is compressed, not encrypted.** The payload is not uniform: 1-bits occur with probability 0.530 in payload A and 0.515 in payload B, and neighbouring bits correlate. The bytes `AA`, `55`, `D5` and `6A` (many bit transitions) are under-represented and `F0`, `E0`, `C0`, `78` and `66` are over-represented. A modern cipher would give a flat 0.500 with no correlation, so this is more likely a compressed bitstream. The profile of over- and under-represented bytes correlates at 0.72–0.79 with dump 8's 1 MB block, so the same compressor probably made both. Payload A changes to payload B between 0x1A6300 and 0x1A6400, where the bit bias briefly drops to 0.44–0.49 and then settles at about 0.515. This is probably the boundary between two objects.
- **No decodable signatures.** `binwalk` finds nothing in the raw or the bit-swapped image. zlib, raw deflate, gzip and LZMA all fail at every offset from 0x001100 to 0x001180. Decompression could not work anyway, because the chip holds only every fourth byte of the stream.

## Device

Unknown device, **low** confidence on what it is. **Medium-high** confidence that it is the same board type and firmware as dump 8. Both chips have the D0↔D2 swap, the same lane-split text and the same payload statistics. The board had a 32-bit data bus with four 5 V MBM29F016 chips per 8 MB bank, which places it in the second half of the 1990s or the early 2000s. This chip's bank starts with a small boot or configuration area and then stores about 8 MB of compressed data, a quarter of which is here. Dump 8's bank holds more compressed data and then diagnostic report records. The two dumps could be bank 0 and bank 1 of one board, with the compressed image running from this bank into dump 8's. They could equally be two units of the same product. Nothing in the readable fragments names a maker or model.

## Open questions

- **The other three lanes.** Neither the code, the strings nor the payload can be rebuilt from one chip. The next step is to dump the other three chips of this bank and interleave them in the four possible orders, choosing the order in which the 0x000E33 strings read as text.
- **Same board as dump 8?** If both chips came from one board, the payload here would continue into dump 8's bank. With all eight lanes, test whether the decompressor's state carries across from 0x1FFFFF here to 0x000000 in dump 8. Dump 8's first two sectors have a bit bias of 0.539 and 0.537, which does not match payload B's 0.515, so a fresh object there is just as likely.
- **Which lane.** Whether this chip is the most or the least significant byte is unknown. Once a second lane exists, the phase of the shared text against dump 8, which holds the same lane phase, would settle the lane number for both chips.
- **Compressor.** The 1-bit bias and neighbouring-bit correlation are a fingerprint. Once the stream is complete, try LZSS/LZ77 variants with bit flags, Huffman-only and arithmetic-coded formats used in embedded firmware of the late 1990s.

## Method

Python (run with `-I`) for the sector and 1 KB entropy maps, byte histograms, run search and per-region 1-bit fraction. An exhaustive search over all 40,320 data-bit permutations, scored against English letter frequency, found the D0↔D2 swap. A within-byte bit-correlation matrix of the payload confirmed it. A bigram model at strides 1, 2, 3, 4 and 8, built from about 3 MB of program strings, estimated the lane stride. Siblings were compared by fill layout, shared lane substrings (length 6 and up) against dump 8's text region and byte-frequency fingerprints. `binwalk` and zlib/LZMA trial decompression were run on the raw and bit-swapped images. Interleaving with dump 7 (the low lane of a two-chip SH bank) gave no text, and dumps 3–6 differ in layout and headers. The figures were drawn with Pillow.
