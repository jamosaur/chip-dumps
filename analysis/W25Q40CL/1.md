This is the complete boot firmware for the 8051 core inside a Realtek RTL837x-family 2.5 GbE switch chip. It holds 191 KB of code and data at the start of the 512 KB flash, and the rest is erased. The strongest evidence is in its strings: the boot banner `RTL8371B start`, the init routine name `init_8373N_8224N_QC`, register commands for "8373/8372" and "8224" SerDes, and Realtek PHY patch routines (`phy_rtl826xb_patch_flow`, `RL6818C_pwr_on_patch_phy_v007`). The code was built with Keil C51. It uses a 16 KB common area plus four 48 KB banks, which are selected through SFR `0x96`.

## Findings

- **Common area is shifted by two bytes.** Flash 0x000002 holds CPU address 0x0000. Three things confirm it. `LJMP 0x2F87` at flash 0x000002 lands on the Keil `STARTUP.A51` sequence at flash 0x002F89 (`78 7F E4 F6 D8 FD 75 81 21 02 2F BC`: clear IDATA, SP=0x21). Every `AJMP` in the bank jump table hits a trampoline only with the shift. The string `init_8373N_8224N_QC…` at flash 0x003689 is loaded as `MOV R2,#36h; MOV R1,#87h`. The two leading bytes `00 40` are therefore not code. They may tell the boot ROM the common-area size (0x40 × 256 = 16 KB), but that is a guess.
- **Banks are not shifted.** Bank n (1–4) occupies flash `0x4000 + (n−1)×0xC000` and appears at CPU 0x4000–0xFFFF. All 31 jump-table targets land just after a `RET` (`22`) and on a plausible function start, such as `90 00 16` = `MOV DPTR,#0016h` at flash 0x004000. Strings in bank 1 are referenced by their bank address (e.g. CPU 0x950B → flash 0x00950B).
- **Bank switching.** Trampolines sit at CPU 0x0100/0x010C/0x0118/0x0124 (flash 0x000102…). Each is `MOV 0BBh,96h; MOV 96h,#n; ACALL 0130h; MOV 96h,0BBh; RET`, where CPU 0x0130 is `CLR A; JMP @A+DPTR`. That makes SFR 0x96 the code-bank register. A one-line helper `MOV 96h,R7; RET` sits at CPU 0x0006. The jump table (CPU 0x0133–0x01CD) has 31 five-byte entries (`90 hi lo 21 xx`): 29 point into bank 1 and 2 into bank 2 (0x69C4, 0x7060). Banks 3 and 4 are reached some other way, probably through the banked `MOVC` helpers that write SFR 0x96 at flash 0x000DF6–0x000E37.
- **Vectors** (CPU addresses): reset → 0x2F87, INT0 (0x03) → 0x34A2, Timer0 (0x0B) → 0x33BD, INT1 (0x13) → 0x346E, extended vectors 0x43 → 0x3452 and 0x4B → 0x3489.
- **main** is at CPU 0x30AE (the startup code's final `LJMP`). It clears some xdata, prints `\nRTL8371B start ` with `printf` (CPU 0x1D36), calls bank 2:0x69C4 (system init), waits (argument 0x0BB8 = 3000), calls bank 1:0xC90F, then loops forever over bank 2:0x7060 and common 0x2377.
- **Keil C51 toolchain.** The startup and `INIT.A51` code are Keil's. The printf strings use the C51-only `%bd`/`%bx` byte modifiers. Code pointers are passed as generic pointers with R3 = `FF`.
- **C51 init table** at CPU 0x271D (flash 0x00271F–0x00287F) holds 47 records. One sets 42 bytes at xdata 0x024F to a ready-made ARP request frame: `FF×6`, source `AA:AA:AA:AA:AA:AA`, EtherType `0806`, sender 192.168.1.1 (`C0 A8 01 01`), target 192.168.1.20 (`C0 A8 01 14`). This is probably a test packet for the port tests.
- **Bank 2 tables** start at 0x010000. There are 134 six-byte `(page, reg, value)` records (pages 0x06, 0x07, 0x1F, 0x21, 0x24, 0x26, 0x28, 0x2E, 0x36), then 16-bit `(reg, data)` pairs. These include the classic Realtek PHY SRAM patch sequence through registers `0xA436`/`0xA438`, plus `0x80xx` parameter writes. Next come 8-byte `(reg, msb, lsb, data)` masked writes, e.g. `A436 000F 0000 81A3`, `A438 000F 0008 0032`. The two formats alternate up to 0x0129C3.
- **Bank 4 patch database.** 0x028000–0x02F9B7 is exactly 1,640 packed 19-byte records matching Realtek's `rtk_hwpatch_t` layout (op, portmask, pagemmd, addr, msb, lsb, data, compare_op, four SRAM words), big-endian. Op counts: 1,518 × op 1 (mostly `0xA436`/`0xA438` PHY SRAM writes), 92 × op 4, 29 × op 2, and 1 × op 200 (data 1000, probably a delay) at 0x02ED2D. The port mask is `0F` in every record but the delay, so the patch targets a four-port PHY.
- **PHY patch versions** named in bank 1 strings: `RL6818C_pwr_on_patch_phy_v007` and `RL6818B_pwr_on_patch_phy_v006`. `RL6818` is probably Realtek's internal project name for the switch's embedded 2.5G PHY, with B and C silicon revisions.
- **UART shell** (bank 2 strings from 0x017501) offers these commands. `wd`/`rd` read and write switch registers. `wd1`/`rd1` take a PHY MMD register (`phyid; mmd_dev; reg`). `wd2`/`rd2` take a SerDes register (`sdsid; page; reg`). `wd3`/`rd3` address registers on the 8224. Others are `rst`, `u0`/`u1` for interrupts, and IOL compliance test modes on ports 0–7: 10M/100M MDI/MDIX, giga modes 1–4 and 2.5G modes 1–6. SerDes tests on chip `3` (8373/8372) or `4` (8224) cover PRBS31/PRBS9, pre/main/post amplitude, TX impedance (z0) and an eye monitor.
- **I2C peripherals:** `rtk_i2c_read`, `getPseReg`/`setPseReg` (a PoE PSE controller register interface) and `getEEReg`/`I2c_EE_test` (EEPROM). These routines exist; the image alone cannot show that a PSE was fitted.
- **SPI-flash driver** in the common area: `__spi_flash_op_wrapper`, `spi_flash_erase`, and the bounds checks `adr[…] is larger than ChipSize`, `flash write area is protected!`. No configuration or log area was written: everything after 0x02F9E0 is erased.
- No version string, build date, MAC address, checksum or image trailer was found. There are no compression or filesystem signatures (binwalk finds nothing).

## Device

Realtek RTL8373N switch with an external RTL8224N quad 2.5G PHY. That gives eight 2.5 GbE ports (the shell asks for ports `0-7`), probably with an SFP/SFP+ uplink on the 8373's SerDes. Confidence: **high** for the Realtek RTL837x 2.5G switch family and 8051 firmware. **Medium** for the RTL8373N + RTL8224N pairing, which comes from `init_8373N_8224N_QC` and the "chip sel: 8373/8372: 3, 8224: 4" help text. **Low** for any specific product. Boards like this are sold as unbranded 8 × 2.5G unmanaged switches with a 512 KB SPI flash. `QC`, `DUT` and `Tool_Channel` in the init error message, together with the large test menu, suggest a Realtek reference or factory-test build. It may also be the stock unmanaged firmware with its debug console left in.

## Open questions

- What do the leading bytes `00 40` mean? Compare with other RTL837x images, or trace the boot ROM's SPI reads on a live board.
- How does code reach banks 3 and 4? Trace the `MOVC` bank helpers at flash 0x000DF6–0x000E37 and the common code at CPU 0x01CE, which references the bank 4 string at CPU 0xB9B8.
- What do the 16 bytes at flash 0x003678 mean (`00 00 00 00 00 00 10 00 00 00 40 00 00 01 C0 00`)? They might be flash geometry or protected-area bounds for the SPI-flash driver; check its callers.
- Patch op codes 2 and 4 and the bank 2 six-byte page numbers are not mapped to register spaces. Compare them with Realtek's open-source `phy_rtl826xb_patch` headers.
- Is `RTL8371B` the internal name of the 8051 SoC family, or a different chip? Check Realtek SDK sources or other RTL837x dumps.

## Method

I mapped entropy and fill per 4 KB sector with Python, hex-dumped the region boundaries, and ran `strings` and `binwalk` (no signatures). I identified the 8051 from `LJMP` vectors and the Keil startup sequence, and disassembled the reset path and `main` with radare2 (`-a 8051`) on the common area cut out with the 2-byte shift. I decoded the Keil bank jump table, the C51 init table and the PHY patch record arrays with short Python scripts, and checked the bank mapping by resolving `MOV R2/R1` string references. The bank map image was rendered with Pillow.
